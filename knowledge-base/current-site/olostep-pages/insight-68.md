### **🎯 The Real Problem: Pitching Too Soon**

One of the most common mistakes I see founders make is rushing to raise before they’re ready—not just in terms of metrics, but in how they _show up_ to investors.

They skip the relationship-building, throw together a last-minute pitch deck, and go straight into “sell mode.” But the truth is: **investors don’t fund decks, they fund people.** People they trust. People they believe in. People they connect with.

### **💡 Here’s What _Get Backed_ Recommends (And What We Discussed This Week)**

If you’re preparing to fundraise (or even thinking about it in the next 12 months), these are the golden nuggets you need to know:

#### **1\. Fundraising = Relationships**

Don’t cold pitch—warm up. Start with coffee chats. Make friends before you make asks. Let investors _see_ your journey and become part of the story.

#### **2\. Know Your Investors**

Do your homework. Who have they funded? What stage do they invest at? What do they care about? Tailor your message to what _matters_ to them.

#### **3\. Tell a Real Story**

Facts inform, but stories sell. Data alone doesn’t move people. Show why _you_ are the right person to solve this problem. Share your passion, grit, and the human side of your startup journey.

#### **4\. Highlight the Right Metrics**

Investors don’t expect millions. They want momentum. Focus on growth signals like retention, referrals, or user feedback. These are signs that the market cares.

#### **5\. Don’t Hide the Risks**

Address the elephant in the room. Investors appreciate transparency. If you show that you’re already thinking through potential obstacles, you become a more credible founder.

#### **6\. Be Ready for Due Diligence**

Have your docs in order, cap table, contracts, financials. Being organized signals professionalism. Bonus points if you have everything ready in a neat little folder or data room.
